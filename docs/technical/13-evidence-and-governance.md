# 13. Evidence, privacy, governance and quality assurance




## 13.1 Evidence-first modeling workflow

- Register the value stream, product boundary, phase coverage, stakeholder categories and scenarios.
- Collect a traditional DFD collaboratively and validate names, flows and stores.
- Attach requirement/business-unit definitions, phase references and critical stakeholder responsibilities.
- Import source contracts, repositories, workflow/pipeline topology, architecture and resource inventory.
- Gather distributed traces, load tests, SLO and incident data, provider invoices and FOCUS exports where available.
- Collect culture/skills/workflow interview information with safe aggregation and explicit instrument limitations.
- Annotate assumptions, uncertainties, alternatives, contradictions and unreviewed claims.
- Run schema and semantic validators; adjudicate critical contradictions; freeze reviewed snapshot.
- Execute independent analysis modules; emit source-linked cost/optimization/diagnostic reports.
- Record interventions and later observed outcomes without mutating the original research baseline.

## 13.2 Review, adjudication and model update triggers


A review package contains the change diff, rule violations, critical inputs, evidence quality, expired fields, covered stakeholder classes and signing roles. Review triggers include new scope, major architecture/pipeline change, new region/vendor, security incident, material price or contract change, SLO regression, altered data retention, major adoption intervention or model/schema revision. Corrections create a new snapshot with provenance; they do not overwrite published t0 research features.


## 13.3 Confidentiality and ethical collection


Source references use least-privilege URLs, hashed pointers or redacted summaries. Secret values, production tokens, employee names, raw salaries, patient/customer data and confidential invoice attachments should not be copied into public graph exports. Culture measurement requires informed participation, safe subgroup publication thresholds and role-based access. Researchers must follow applicable institutional ethics review and jurisdictional privacy rules. Model reports should be designed for organizational learning, not individual employee ranking.


## 13.4 Quality dimensions and interpretation


| Dimension | Operational measure | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage | Scoped critical artifacts/flows/roles/requirements with review | Graph may look complete but omit real work |
| Trace correctness | Independent review and tool-source verification of sampled edges | False linkage between code, cost and business capability |
| Inter-rater agreement | Cohen/Fleiss kappa or agreement for discrete annotations; ICC for numeric ratings | Subjective labels vary by analyst |
| Timeliness | Fraction of relevant annotations within freshness policy | Stale measurements used for new architecture |
| Source quality | A/B/C/U evidence grade and contradictions by domain | Estimates presented as primary facts |
| Decision utility | Task completion, action precision, adoption of feasible recommendations | Complex model with no action benefit |
| Collection burden | Analyst/stakeholder hours per critical flow/value stream | Over-modeling with low marginal benefit |


## 13.5 AI data, provenance and security-of-operations protocol

- Source discovery: register all model, tool, data, provider and human authority boundaries; record unobserved shadow AI use as an issue without intrusive employee monitoring.
- Threat analysis: evaluate prompt injection and content trust, model/data/memory poisoning, excessive agency, tool misuse, credential overreach, insecure inter-agent messages, cascading failures and unaudited persistent context; use current OWASP guidance as an external taxonomy, not automatic compliance.
- AI system impact: document intended and foreseeable misuse, affected stakeholders, human oversight/appeal, consent where relevant, impact review and review cadence; map to ISO/IEC 42005 and NIST AI RMF without claiming certification.
- Data rights: record origin, retention, geographic processing, training use, retrieval permissions and deletion; document provider contractual data-use terms and model/embedding licensing.
- Trace privacy: prefer run/model/tool IDs, counts, hashes and redacted/sampled traces over full raw prompts; segregate metadata from sensitive text and give evidence owners access and retention controls.
- Change triggers: alter model/vendor, prompt, tool scopes, RAG corpus, training dataset, serving location, price tier, regulation, affected population or business-use definition → re-review linked assumptions and controls.
- Kill and remediation: verify agent stop, credential revocation, queued action cancellation, compensating transaction and incident escalation with test artifacts; never trust a textual model promise to stop.

The proposed language cannot guarantee all security threats are represented, that model outputs are truthful, or that a provider follows its declared policies. Independent system/organizational controls and assessments remain necessary.
